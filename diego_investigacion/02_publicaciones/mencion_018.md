#Santander: The Moment Between Potential and Decision — Chapter II
By Jorge Arturo Acevedo Alarid, Ph.D

There is something you feel in Barrancabermeja and along the Magdalena River that numbers alone cannot explain: a mix of pride and unfinished momentum.

Infrastructure exists. Energy exists. Talent exists. But coordination remains inconsistent.

The Magdalena River—once envisioned as a competitive logistics corridor—remains underutilized. The weakening of structured river logistics is not just a sector issue; it reflects a broader challenge: execution without alignment loses scale.

Colombia is entering a defining political moment with the 2026 elections. This is not just a democratic process—it is a confidence signal for capital markets, investors, and long-term projects. Regions that depend solely on national direction risk slowing down. Regions that organize themselves move forward regardless of cycles.

And here is where Santander has a real opportunity.

I saw one of the most powerful examples at Campuslands, led by Diego Tarazona. This is not traditional education. It is an immersive, high-performance training model where young people are prepared in software development, AI, English, and professional skills—and connected to real jobs in months. That is economic transformation. That is what I call “mind-facturing”—turning talent into productive capital.

From my Holistic Vision Model (#Hvm®) and Holistic Infrastructure Model (#Him®), the path forward is not more analysis—it is structured execution:

– Prioritize 5–7 anchor projects that integrate water, logistics, energy, and talent.
– Establish a technical governance body (PMO) that ensures continuity beyond political cycles.
– Convert the Magdalena River into a real economic corridor, not a recurring narrative.
– Scale Zona Franca Santander into a regional export and services hub.
– Align academia, industry, and government into a single execution framework.

As Michael Porter reminds us, “The essence of strategy is choosing what not to do.” Santander must choose focus over dispersion.

I return with deep admiration—for its people, their discipline, their warmth, their strength. Santander is tierra berraca. That is not folklore—it is a competitive advantage.

But in the next decade, character will not be enough.

The regions that will lead are those that align vision, execution, and trust.

Santander already has everything it needs.

Now comes the only decision that matters:

To act—together.
